Over the last couple of days, work on the ADS-B Tracker has concerned something rather less visually exciting than aircraft tracks, position-density plots or animated observation replays.

It has concerned a queue.

As aircraft messages are received and processed, requests to persist observations are placed into a first-in, first-out queue. A serial writer then applies them to the back-end database in the same order.

The queue is now backed by a durable file-based spool. If the application stops, the machine loses power or database processing is deliberately deferred, pending entries remain safely on disk until they can be replayed.

That is useful engineering in its own right.

Its real significance, however, becomes clearer when the software is considered from the observer's point of view.

## The observer beside the receiver

Imagine an observation session at an airport.

The receiver and laptop have been running for several hours. Aircraft have come and gone, tens-of-thousands of messages have been processed and many observations have already been written to the database.

Others remain queued.

The observer, meanwhile, has been sitting beside the equipment. Perhaps it is getting cold. Perhaps it has begun to rain. The light may be fading, the airport viewing area may be closing or there may be a bus or train to catch.

The observation is finished.

An application that insists upon completely draining its database queue before it can shut down might nevertheless require the observer to remain there for many more minutes—or, with a sufficiently large backlog and slow storage, much longer.

The software would be technically protecting the data while practically preventing the observer from leaving.

That is not a good trade!

## Two legitimate endings

The Tracker Hub now makes the choice explicit when a session ends with database writes still pending.

{% include fullwidth-image.html assets=page.assets img=page.session_stop_queue %}

The observer can choose **Flush now** and wait while the queued entries are written to SQLite.

Or they can choose **Later**.

Choosing **Later** does not abandon the observations. The entries remain in the durable spool, still ordered and waiting to be processed. The equipment can be shut down and packed away, and the queue can be replayed later using a separate _Spool Replayer_.

Both choices are valid.

At home, with a small queue and no immediate reason to stop, allowing the database writer to finish may be the obvious decision.

At the end of a long airport session, in worsening weather or with transport waiting, retaining the queue may be far more sensible.

The durable spool turns that from a risky interruption into an ordinary part of the workflow.

## Pending does not mean lost

Without durable storage, an unwritten database entry is vulnerable. Shutting down before it has been processed may mean losing it.

The file-backed queue changes the meaning of **pending**.

An observation can now be:

1. Received and interpreted by the tracker
2. Safely accepted into the durable spool
3. Applied to the SQLite database

Those stages often follow one another immediately, but they no longer have to be completed together.

The important distinction is that an entry waiting in the spool has already been captured safely. It has not yet become part of the analytical database, but neither has it disappeared.

That allows observation and database processing to proceed at different times.

## Observation and housekeeping

The timing of an observation is governed by the outside world.

Aircraft activity changes. Weather deteriorates. Batteries run down. Daylight fades. Access to a site ends. Public transport runs to a timetable.

Database processing is different. It is necessary, but it is housekeeping performed on data that has already been collected.

The observer may need to be present for the first of those activities. They do not need to remain exposed to the conditions merely to supervise the second.

This suggests a useful separation:

> Capture belongs to the observation. Database replay belongs to processing.

They may happen together, and under normal circumstances they usually will. The continuous writer can drain the queue while tracking remains active, and the remaining entries can be flushed when the session stops.

But durability means that the end of the observation no longer has to wait for the end of database processing.

## When stop has to mean stop

There is a subtle domain distinction here.

Stopping an observation session means that the deliberate period of observation has ended. It does not necessarily mean that every internal operation resulting from that observation has also completed.

The session can therefore be closed while queued database work remains.

Even an in-progress stop-time flush can be cancelled safely. Entries already written remain processed, while the current unacknowledged entry and everything behind it remain in the spool for later replay.

Cancelling the flush does not cancel the observation.

That seems obvious when expressed in those terms, but it required the software to distinguish the lifecycle of the observing session from the lifecycle of its persistence work.

> The observer decides when the observation is complete

The database queue should not overrule them.

## Human-centred engineering

The receiver may be capturing the radio signals. The software may be parsing messages, maintaining aircraft state and recording positions. Most of the mechanical work is automated.

The observer is still part of the system.

They selected the place and time. They brought the equipment, started the session and remained with it. They are the one sitting in the wind and rain while the machine works.

Designing an observational tool therefore means considering more than whether its algorithms and storage mechanisms are correct. It means considering what the application asks of the person using it in real conditions.

A technically safe shutdown process that requires an observer to remain unnecessarily in the field is not entirely successful.

A durable queue provides the technical solution, but the important design decision is to place control in the observer's hands.

## The less visible foundations

Position-density plots and animated replays are among the more immediately rewarding results of the ADS-B tracking project. They expose aircraft paths, traffic corridors and the developing shape of the receiver's view.

The queue is less visible.

Nevertheless, those later visualisations depend upon the reliability of the data beneath them. Ordered message processing, durable capture and recoverable database writing provide part of the foundation upon which the more interesting analysis rests.

This work may not produce a dramatic new plot, but it makes the observational instrument more dependable and more practical to use.

More importantly, it acknowledges a simple principle:

> Never make the observer endure field conditions for work the computer can safely finish later

Sometimes good observational software is not about asking the machine to do more.

Sometimes it is about allowing the observer to go home.
