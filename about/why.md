# Why I Keep Field Notes

{% include fullwidth-image.html assets=page.assets img=page.chapelfield %}

The photograph above is of Chapelfield in Greater Manchester. It’s the landscape in which I was born and grew up. I sometimes wonder whether my habit of paying attention to the natural world began there.

Retirement creates freedom, but freedom itself does not provide direction. Field Notes Journal is my attempt to use that freedom constructively: to pursue long-term interests, investigate questions that matter to me, build a body of work that reflects my curiosity and effort, and make the results available to anyone who may find them interesting, useful or enjoyable.

Field Notes Journal exists because I find the process of observing, investigating and learning worthwhile in its own right. Sharing that work is an important part of it, not because success is measured by the size of an audience, but because ideas become more valuable when they are available to others. If something here encourages someone to look more closely, pursue a new interest, or ask a question of their own, then that is a genuine pleasure.

Software plays an important part in that practice. During my working life, it was principally the thing I made. Since retiring, it has increasingly become one of the ways I investigate the things that interest me: a means of recording observations, modelling processes, testing ideas, revealing patterns and making what I learn visible to others.

Ultimately, Field Notes Journal is a personal effort to turn curiosity into something tangible and shareable: to explore carefully, learn honestly, create thoughtfully, and make the results freely available to anyone who wishes to follow along. I hope that the work not only communicates what I have discovered, but also reflects the way it was pursued—with curiosity, care, and a willingness to revise my understanding wherever the evidence leads.
