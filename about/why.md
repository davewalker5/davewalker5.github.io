# Why I Keep Field Notes

{% include fullwidth-image.html assets=page.assets img=page.chapelfield %}

The photograph above is of Chapelfield in Greater Manchester. It’s the landscape in which I was born and grew up. I sometimes wonder whether my habit of paying attention to the natural world began there.

Retirement creates freedom, but freedom itself does not provide direction. Field Notes Journal is my attempt to use that freedom constructively: to pursue long-term interests, investigate questions that matter to me, build a body of work that reflects my curiosity and effort, and make the results available to anyone who may find them interesting, useful or enjoyable.

The value of the work does not depend on the size of its audience. The observations, investigations and learning are worthwhile in their own right. Yet there is satisfaction in knowing that the results are freely available to all, and that they might encourage someone else to look more closely, explore a new interest, or follow a question of their own.

Ultimately, Field Notes Journal is a personal effort to turn curiosity into something lasting: to explore carefully, learn honestly, create thoughtfully, and share the results freely with anyone who wishes to follow along. I hope that the work not only communicates what I have discovered, but also reflects the way it was pursued—with curiosity, care, and a willingness to revise my understanding wherever the evidence leads.
