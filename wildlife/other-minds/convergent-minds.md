## Convergent Minds

The deepest significance of cephalopods is evolutionary.

Our last common ancestor was a comparatively simple animal. Vertebrates and cephalopods subsequently evolved large nervous systems and sophisticated behaviour largely independently.

Yet both lineages arrived at abilities including learning, memory, flexible behaviour and exploration, alongside evidence that has prompted serious questions about complex subjective experience..

This convergence suggests that mind is not tied to one particular nervous-system architecture. Evolution can produce complex cognition by radically different routes.

The octopus is therefore interesting precisely because it is **not** much like us. It represents something close to an independent evolutionary experiment in building a mind.
