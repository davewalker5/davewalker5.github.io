## Sentience, Consciousness and Moral Importance

This leads to an important distinction between:

**capacity for cognition ≠ capacity for experience**

Greater cognitive sophistication should not automatically imply greater moral worth. That position would have deeply problematic implications even within our own species.

Sentience provides a better foundation for moral concern: if an organism can have experiences that are good or bad for it, then it has welfare interests. Greater cognitive complexity may alter the *character* of those experiences and the kinds of needs an organism possesses, but it need not determine whether those experiences matter morally.

Sentience may also precede what we normally call consciousness. Basic subjective experience could arise from the sensory-motor processes through which an organism engages with its world, while the richly integrated, reflective consciousness familiar to humans may be a later evolutionary development.
