## The Unity of Experience

{% include fullwidth-image.html assets=page.assets img=page.sensory_integration %}

Human consciousness normally feels unified: information from different senses and different parts of the brain is integrated into a single experienced world.

Split-brain cases suggest that this unity is not as indivisible as it appears. When communication between the cerebral hemispheres is disrupted, aspects of perception, knowledge and action can become partially separated.

The octopus raises an even more radical version of this question. Its nervous system is distributed from the outset rather than being a centrally organised vertebrate system that has subsequently been divided.

This raises the possibility that conscious experience need not always have the strongly unified character familiar to us. There may be more than one way of organising a subjective point of view.
