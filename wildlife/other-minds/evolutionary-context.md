## Minds Evolve in Bodies and Environments

The origins of mind lie in the relationship between organisms and their environments. Even simple cells sense conditions, respond to them and communicate chemically. With increasing multicellular complexity, signalling within the organism becomes increasingly important, eventually producing nervous systems capable of coordinating perception and action.

One useful way of understanding the early evolution of nervous systems is as systems for **sensory-motor coordination**: sensing what is happening and organising an appropriate response.

Predation seems to have been particularly important in driving this process. Predator and prey exert reciprocal evolutionary pressures upon one another, favouring better sensing, mobility, behavioural flexibility and increasingly sophisticated nervous systems.

Cognition is consequently shaped by:

- The ecological problems an organism faces
- Its sensory systems
- Its body plan
- Its evolutionary history

Together these determine the particular world available to an organism and the problems its nervous system has evolved to solve.
