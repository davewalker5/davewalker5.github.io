## Another Route to Intelligence

Cephalopods are especially interesting because their evolutionary lineage separated from ours extremely early. Complex nervous systems therefore evolved largely independently in vertebrates and cephalopods.

This makes the octopus not a primitive approximation of a vertebrate mind, but an example of a substantially different evolutionary solution to similar problems.

Its nervous system illustrates this particularly clearly. Much neural processing is distributed through the arms rather than being concentrated entirely within the central brain. The arms have considerable sensory and motor autonomy while remaining coordinated as parts of the whole animal.

{% include fullwidth-image.html assets=page.assets img=page.octopus_nervous_system %}

The result challenges familiar ideas about the boundary between **self and non-self**. An octopus arm is a partly autonomous component of the animal’s behavioural system.

This raises a wider question: how much of what we assume to be essential to having a mind is genuinely necessary, and how much simply reflects the particular architecture of the vertebrate nervous system?
