## Intelligence Is Not a Single Scale

Animal intelligence is difficult to assess because experimental tests inevitably embody assumptions about what counts as an appropriate problem and an appropriate response.

An apparent failure may therefore reflect a mismatch between the experiment and the animal rather than an absence of cognitive ability. Differences in perception, motivation, ecology and body plan can all affect performance.

A task designed around vision, object manipulation or a particular reward may be straightforward for one species and ecologically meaningless to another. Poor performance can therefore reflect the design of the experiment as much as the cognitive capacities of its subject.

Individual temperament matters too. The differing behaviour of particular experimental octopuses — especially the wonderfully troublesome Charles — illustrates that an animal is not simply an interchangeable representative of its species.

There is therefore a strong anthropocentric danger in treating human-style problem solving as the universal standard of intelligence.
