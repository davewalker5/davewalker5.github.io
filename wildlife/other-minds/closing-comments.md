## Closing Comments

*Other Minds* begins with the basic biological problems of sensing, signalling and acting and ultimately arrives at questions about intelligence, sentience, consciousness and the nature of the self.

The connections seem to form a progression:

**environment → sensing and action → nervous systems → cognition → subjective experience → consciousness**

But evolution has not followed this path only once, nor has it produced only one solution.

Cephalopods demonstrate that complex minds can arise from bodies, nervous systems and evolutionary histories very different from our own. Their greatest philosophical significance may therefore be that they force us to separate the concept of **mind** from the concept of **human-like mind**.

The question left behind is perhaps the most interesting one:

> **What are the minimum ingredients from which a subjective point of view can emerge, and how many different ways can evolution assemble them?**
