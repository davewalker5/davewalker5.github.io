# Ferry 'Cross the Mersey

No visit to Liverpool would feel quite complete without a trip on the Mersey Ferry, so during a visit to the city in November 2026 we took a journey aboard the _Royal Daffodil_, setting out from Pier Head to see Liverpool from the perspective of the river that has shaped so much of its history and character.

The waterfront is an impressive sight from the water, with the "Three Graces" - the Royal Liver Building, Cunard Building and Port of Liverpool Building - standing alongside the more modern architecture of the ferry terminal and the Museum of Liverpool.

Together with the nearby Royal Albert Dock complex, these historic buildings are reminders of the maritime trade and industry that helped make Liverpool one of Britain's great ports. The Mersey remains a working river, busy with commercial shipping, with an active port extending northwards along the Liverpool waterfront towards Seaforth, where the estuary opens out into Liverpool Bay and the Irish Sea.

Across the river from Pier Head, the _Stena Embla_, one of the Stena Line ferries operating the regular service between Birkenhead and Belfast, docks at the Twelve Quays terminal and further along the Birkenhead waterfront are the shipyards of _Cammell Laird_, still actively engaged in shipbuilding, maintenance and repair, and, very appropriately, the yard chosen to build the _Royal Daffodil_ on which we were sailing.

_Ferry 'Cross the Mersey_ is a short film combining moving footage and photographs taken during the journey. Beginning with the _Royal Daffodil_ leaving Pier Head, it follows the Liverpool waterfront, pauses to admire the Three Graces, crosses to Seacombe on the Wirral shore and explores some of the commercial shipping and shipyard activity along the river before returning to Pier Head.

And the title? It's almost impossible to contemplate a journey across the Mersey without hearing _Gerry and the Pacemakers_ somewhere in the back of your mind so it's a nod to their 1964 song, and to a river crossing that remains part of Liverpool's identity.
