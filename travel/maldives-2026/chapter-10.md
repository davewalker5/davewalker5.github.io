We'd barely arrived at Moofushi before the sharks began appearing.

On our first day, a blacktip reef shark passed the water villa. Soon there were further sightings: one beneath a walkway, juveniles patrolling the shallows, and occasional larger individuals moving through the lagoon.

Then, on the morning of 14th October, we saw something much less familiar.

## Something unusual on the way to breakfast

Walking to breakfast, we noticed a group of blacktip reef sharks, each roughly a metre long, interacting in the shallow water.

My immediate impression was that the behaviour might have been courtship-related. There was enough interaction between the animals to make it feel different from the solitary cruising we'd already seen.

I wrote in the diary that I felt privileged to witness it. I still do.

But *courtship-like* is an observation and an interpretation, not a confirmed identification of the behaviour. Without a clearer record of the sequence, and without specialist advice, I'd be reluctant to be more definite.

## Familiar sharks, unfamiliar lives

Blacktip reef sharks are conspicuous inhabitants of tropical shallows. Their dark-tipped fins can make identification relatively straightforward, and the sheltered water around the island offers frequent chances to see them.

Yet recognising the species is not the same as understanding what its members are doing. A shark travelling from one place to another tells us relatively little. A group of sharks interacting invites questions about social behaviour, competition, mating and context.

Even the simplest question — why were they together just there, just then? — is worth asking.

## Looking again

One advantage of taking photographs and video is the opportunity to review an event later, when the excitement has passed. In this case, the diary preserves what we saw and how it struck us, but not enough detail for a firm behavioural conclusion.

I'd be interested in comparing any surviving images or footage with descriptions of documented blacktip reef shark behaviour. Useful questions would include whether there was contact or biting, whether one animal appeared to follow another, and whether the group stayed together or dispersed.

Those details matter more than a dramatic label.

## Wildlife on the doorstep

The encounter was also characteristic of Moofushi in another way. We weren't on an organised wildlife excursion. We were simply walking to breakfast.

Later that week a juvenile blacktip would patrol the shallows near the restaurant, while other sharks appeared beside the villas or under the jetties.

The lagoon is their habitat. We happen to be able to observe it from remarkably comfortable surroundings.

I think that morning's encounter will remain one of my favourite small mysteries from the visit — not because I can explain it completely, but because it reminded me how much more there is to learn about creatures that have become, in their own way, familiar neighbours.
