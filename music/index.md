# Music

Music is one of the great constants in my life.

This section is not intended to be a collection of reviews, ratings or recommendations. There are plenty of places better suited to that. Instead, these are field notes from listening, collecting and occasionally looking at the collection itself as a source of data.

Sometimes that may mean writing about a particular record or performance that has stopped me in my tracks. Sometimes it may mean highlighting an unusual pressing, an interesting sleeve, a production oddity or simply a record with a story attached to it.

At other times, it may mean stepping back and looking at the collection as a whole.

I wrote my own **Music Catalogue** application to manage the records I own. As well as the usual catalogue information, it allows me to tag recordings stylistically and to explore the collection through reporting and visualisation — looking at things such as genre, energy, intimacy, warmth, collecting depth and buying patterns.

That has made the collection interesting in two different ways:

- as a collection of individual physical objects, performances and memories
- as a body of data which sometimes reveals patterns I had never consciously noticed

Both feel worth exploring.

## From the Collection

Individual records that deserve a closer look.

These may be unusual pressings, label variations, production errors, particularly beautiful editions, records with an interesting history, or simply things in the collection that make me smile.

A 1956 original Liberty pressing of *Julie Is Her Name*, for example, is interesting not only because of Julie London and the music, but because my copy comes from the first production run and carries a wonderfully human labelling error: side A is labelled B, and side B is labelled A.

Those are the sorts of details I enjoy.

## Listening Notes

Not reviews.

Just occasional notes about music that catches my attention: a performance, an arrangement, a voice, a moment in a recording, or the simple experience of putting on a record and being reminded why music matters so much.

Sometimes the interest is in the music itself. Sometimes it is in what the music brings to mind — memories, associations, emotions, or thoughts that might otherwise have remained unexpressed.

Sometimes one song is enough.

## The Collection as Data

A record collection accumulates gradually, often without any deliberate overall plan.

That makes it interesting to ask what patterns have emerged.

Using the reporting tools in Music Catalogue, I can explore questions such as:

- Which genres dominate the collection?
- Do I tend to collect particular artists deeply or range widely?
- What stylistic characteristics recur most often?
- Where do different parts of the collection cluster?
- Do particular retailers or sources correspond with different collecting patterns?
- Does the collection I have actually match the collection I imagine I have?

The answers are sometimes obvious.

Sometimes they are not.

## Why Music?

Because music does something that very few other things can do.

It can attach itself to memories, alter the atmosphere of a room, make an ordinary evening feel significant, and occasionally stop thought altogether for a few minutes.

It can also evoke things that are difficult to put into words. A familiar piece of music can suddenly become an encounter with memory, ageing, loss, gratitude or the simple awareness of time passing.

Sometimes the experience is joyful. Sometimes it is profoundly moving. Sometimes it manages to be both at once.

I find that increasingly interesting — not just the music, but what happens when I listen to it.

These pages are simply notes made along the way.
